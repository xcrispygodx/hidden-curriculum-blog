Operation Northwoods Declassified:
False Flags the World Never Forgot

12 MIN READ • MARCH 2026 • THE HIDDEN CURRICULUM

In 1962, the U.S. Joint Chiefs of Staff drafted a series of proposals—Operation Northwoods—calling for false flag terrorist attacks against American citizens to justify military intervention in Cuba. The proposals were approved by every member of the Joint Chiefs and sent to Secretary of Defense Robert McNamara. They were rejected by President John F. Kennedy.

JFK was assassinated 18 months later.

◆ The Proposal

The Northwoods document, declassified in 1998, proposed a chilling menu of options:

• "We could blow up a U.S. ship in Guantanamo Bay and blame Cuba"

• "a 'Cuban' D-23 drone aircraft could be destroyed at a calculated distance from land and permitted to be found by U.S. or Cuban forces"

• "We could develop a Communist Cuban terror campaign in the Miami area, in other Florida cities, and even in Washington"

• "It is possible to create an incident which will demonstrate convincingly that a Cuban aircraft has attacked and shot down a chartered civil airliner"

◆ The JFK Connection

Northwoods was part of a broader coup attempt within the U.S. national security apparatus. The Joint Chiefs resented Kennedy's refusal to authorize a full invasion of Cuba after the Bay of Pigs. They saw him as weak on communism. They were prepared to manufacture an act of war.

⚠ Historical Timeline

  • March 1962: Northwoods draft completed and signed by General Lyman Lemnitzer
  • April 1962: McNamara rejects the proposal
  • September 1962: Kennedy publicly confronts Soviet missiles in Cuba
  • November 22, 1963: JFK assassinated in Dallas. Only months after his second term began
  • 1998: Northwoods document declassified
  • 2017: Remaining JFK assassination documents partially released — 2,800 still classified
  • 2025: National Archives confirms 1,100 additional documents will not be released until 2039
