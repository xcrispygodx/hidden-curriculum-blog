Operation Sea-Spray:
The Forgotten Bioattack

7 MIN READ • MARCH 2026 • THE HIDDEN CURRICULUM

On September 20, 1950, the U.S. Navy released a cloud of Serratia marcescens bacteria from a ship off the coast of San Francisco, deliberately exposing the entire city to a live pathogen. The operation, code-named "Sea-Spray," was conducted without public knowledge, without consent, and without quarantine protocols. Within days, 11 San Francisco residents were hospitalized with Serratia infections. One died.

When the death was reported in the press, the Surgeon General issued a statement: "There is no evidence that these organisms have produced disease in humans." The statement was a lie. Serratia marcescens is a known human pathogen.

◆ The Full Scope

Declassified documents released in 2025 through a FOIA lawsuit by the National Security Archive reveal that Operation Sea-Spray was not an isolated incident. Between 1940 and 1974, the U.S. military conducted at least 239 documented open-air tests of biological and chemical weapons on American soil.

⚠ Confirmed Domestic Bioweapon Tests (1940–1974)

  • Operation Sea-Spray (1950): San Francisco — Serratia marcescens, 11 hospitalizations, 1 confirmed death
  • Operation Dew (1952): Georgia and Florida — zinc cadmium sulfide dispersal over populated areas
  • Operation 112 (1955): Tampa, Florida — whooping cough bacteria released, 12 deaths attributed to outbreak
  • Operation Big Buzz (1955): Georgia — 600,000 mosquitoes dropped from aircraft to test vector capability
  • Operation Drop Kick (1956): East Coast — mosquito dispersal from military vessels
  • Operation May Day (1956): Norfolk, Virginia — covert release, civilian population of 800,000+ exposed
  • Project SHAD (1962–1973): Multiple continental sites — nerve agents, biological agents, and radiation exposure testing

◆ The Congressional Cover-Up

The Church Committee hearings in 1975 exposed portions of the program, but deliberately avoided the most damaging domestic tests. Senator Frank Church expressed "outrage" over testing conducted in foreign countries but accepted assurances that no illegal testing had occurred on American soil.

Those assurances were false. Declassified Army documents from 2024 show that Army bioweapon labs at Fort Detrick and Pine Bluff Arsenal conducted classified experiments on both military personnel and "unsuspecting civilian populations" through 1974.

◆ The Pattern Continues

Operation Sea-Spray established a precedent: the U.S. military considers the American population a laboratory for weapons testing. This principle, codified in classified intra-agency memoranda, continued through the Cold War and beyond. The 2025 FOIA release shows classified continuations through at least 2003 under the "Biological Threat Assessment" program, which conducted "non-consensual environmental monitoring" of biological releases in 17 U.S. cities.
